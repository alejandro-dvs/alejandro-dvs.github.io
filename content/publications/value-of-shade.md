abstract: "We estimate mortality effects of urban canopy loss, instrumenting with nearby forest insect outbreaks. Where outbreaks measurably reduce canopy and remaining shade is limited, loss raises mortality by 6.3 percent during hot, dry, sunny April–June but has no detectable effect during the also-hot, cloudier July–September. Effects concentrate in cardiovascular mortality, span working-age and older adults, and appear regressive by insurance status. Converging evidence points to shade as the primary channel. When heat and solar exposure coincide, losing this shared service implies about 120 additional deaths per year. Conserving existing canopy is thus a valuable component of urban heat adaptation."
